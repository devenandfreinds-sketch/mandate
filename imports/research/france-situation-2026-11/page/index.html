Public schools: large classes, and an offer far below what students asked for

The student protests are about conditions more than curriculum: teachers who are absent and not replaced, very large classes, buildings in poor repair and the stress of the Parcoursup university admissions platform. OECD data back the complaint about class size. France cut primary classes sharply after 2017, but its lower secondary classes grew and are among the largest in the OECD.

Verdict: a short-term response

— of the — measures are temporary relief or postponements. Another — would last but are small or cover part of the problem, and most start in 2027 or later, after the presidential election.

Only one is a full structural change that is going ahead, the replacement of Parcoursup. The one plan aimed at the long-run problem, the 2027 budget, was voted down, and it pulls against the concessions: every euro promised to schools or wages makes the —% deficit target harder.

Why it is not a long-term solution

  • It treats the symptoms. Releasing fuel stocks and bringing forward a wage rise ease this winter. They do not change growth, energy dependence or how pay is set.
  • The money is not there. The school package relies on loans and on promises dated 2027 to 2030. Students got a small fraction of the university places they asked for.
  • The hard choices are postponed. The pension age, public-sector pay and the budget itself have each been pushed back, mostly past the election.
  • There is no majority to carry a long-term plan. A government whose budget loses — to — cannot commit its successors.

What a long-term answer would need: a budget that parliament actually votes, a settled pension rule, multi-year funding for teachers and school buildings paid from the budget, and a return to growth. None of these is in place today.
