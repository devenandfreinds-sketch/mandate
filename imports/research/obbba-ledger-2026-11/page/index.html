Big Beautiful Bill Ledger
Public Law 119-21 · signed July 4, 2025 · government sources only · figures as of November 15, 2026

The One Big Beautiful Bill, in numbers

The law cut taxes by $4.5 trillion over ten years, cut direct spending by $1.1 trillion, and gave immigration enforcement its largest budget in history. Every figure here comes from a federal agency, Congress’s own scorekeepers or its research and audit arms.

The cost

$4.5 trillion in tax cuts, $1.1 trillion in spending cuts

The Congressional Budget Office and the Joint Committee on Taxation scored the law against the budget as it stood in January 2025. The spending cuts cover about a quarter of the lost revenue. Borrowing covers the rest, and the interest on that borrowing adds another $718 billion.

Who gains, who loses

The bottom tenth loses $1,214 a year. The top tenth gains $13,622.

CBO combined the tax cuts with the Medicaid and food-assistance cuts and measured the net effect on each tenth of households. The bottom two tenths end up with less. Everyone from the middle up gains, and the gains grow with income.

The one percent

The top 1% get 24% of the tax cut. The bottom 60% get 13%.

The Joint Committee on Taxation, Congress’s official tax scorekeeper, split the 2027 tax cut by income group. Of $545 billion, $131.5 billion goes to the top 1%, who start at about $526,000 in size-adjusted income. The top 0.1%, above about $2.04 million, get $59.9 billion.

  • $113.6BShare of the 2027 cut going to tax units with income of $1 million or more, 20.8% of the total (JCT).
  • 31.1% → 27.8%Average federal tax rate for the $1 million-and-over group, before and after. For all taxpayers it falls from 20.9% to 18.7% (JCT).
  • +$0.5BTax units under $15,000 pay more in total in 2027, not less, a 9.7% increase. By 2029 the $15,000–$30,000 group also pays slightly more (JCT).
  • $15MEstate and gift tax exemption per person from 2026, indexed. It had been set to fall to about $7.14 million (CRS).
  • 37%Top income tax rate made permanent, along with the 20% deduction for pass-through business income and immediate write-offs for equipment bought after January 19, 2025 (CRS).
The president

How Donald Trump stands to benefit

No government body has estimated what the law saves Trump personally, so there is no figure to report. His tax returns are not public. His annual financial disclosure to the Office of Government Ethics, certified June 29, 2026, lists assets and income in broad ranges and does not show tax owed.

Health and food

Medicaid loses $990 billion. Food assistance rolls are down 5.5 million.

The Medicaid cuts mostly start in 2027. Food assistance changed first, and the Agriculture Department’s own counts show enrollment falling every month since the law was signed.

  • −5.5MDrop in SNAP recipients from July 2025 (41.6 million) to July 2026 (36.1 million), a 13.3% fall. USDA’s data do not record why people leave, and enrollment was already drifting down before the law.
  • 10.0MMore people uninsured in 2034 from the health provisions, 7.5 million of them from the Medicaid changes (CBO, via CRS).
  • 2.4MFewer people on SNAP in an average month from the work-requirement changes alone, as CBO projected (via CRS).
  • 80 hoursMonthly work, study or service required of Medicaid expansion adults from December 31, 2026, with eligibility checks every six months from January 1, 2027 (CRS).
Immigration enforcement

ICE got seven years of budget in one bill

The law gave the Department of Homeland Security $191 billion to spend through fiscal 2029. ICE received $74.85 billion, including $45 billion for detention. Detention, arrests, deportations and deaths in custody have all risen since. Some of that rise began in early 2025, before the money arrived.

  • 39,314 → 67,204ICE’s average daily detained population, January 20, 2025 to April 1, 2026, up 71% (GAO, from ICE data).
  • 134 → 239Authorized detention facilities over the same period, up 78%. ICE plans a further $38 billion acquisition program, partly to convert warehouses (GAO).
  • 491,000ICE arrests in fiscal 2026, nearly 475,000 of them administrative. DHS reports this and the 483,200 deportations as agency records and a result it sought.
  • $1.3BCeiling on the contract for Camp East Montana, a 5,000-bed tent facility at Fort Bliss. GAO found up to $11.5 million wasted in its first two weeks and about $7.5 million more paid for meals nobody needed.
  • No inspectionICE housed people at Camp East Montana without the pre-occupancy inspection its own policy requires. The site opened without perimeter cameras, outdoor recreation space or room for attorney visits (GAO).
  • HomicideA coroner’s ruling on one January 2026 death there, by asphyxia after staff used force. The contractor did not file the required reports and evidence was missing or destroyed (GAO). DHS agreed with all four GAO recommendations.

There is no official national count of shootings by immigration agents in the sources used here, so this version has no shootings figure. Earlier drafts relied on news-based trackers for that.

The case for it

What the administration points to

The Treasury analyzed the 2026 filing season, the first under the new law. Its numbers count who claimed each break, which is a different question from who got the most dollars.

  • 97%Of filers got a tax cut compared with letting the 2017 cuts expire. 96% of them earned under $200,000 and nearly 70% under $100,000 (Treasury).
  • $82BIndividual relief claimed through the April deadline from the law’s new provisions (Treasury).
  • $815Average cut for filers earning $50,000–$100,000 who claimed one of the new deductions; over $1,250 for those at $100,000–$200,000 (Treasury).
  • $7,000Average tips deduction, claimed by 7.5 million filers, 90% of them under $100,000. The average overtime deduction was over $3,100 (Treasury).
  • $3,276Average refund in the 2026 filing season, up 11.5% from $2,939 (IRS).
Schedule

What has taken effect and what is coming

  • Jul 4, 2025Signed. Tax changes apply to the 2025 tax year. Homeland security funding becomes available.
  • Sep 30, 2025Clean vehicle tax credit ends for vehicles acquired after this date.
  • Jan 1, 2026$15 million estate and gift exemption begins.
  • Dec 31, 2026Medicaid work requirement begins for expansion adults aged 19–64.
  • Jan 1, 2027Medicaid eligibility checks every six months. States start paying 75% of SNAP administrative costs in fiscal 2027.
  • Fiscal 2028States start paying a share of SNAP benefit costs.
  • End of 2028Tips, overtime, senior and car-loan deductions expire.
  • 2030State and local tax deduction cap returns to $10,000 from $40,000.
Sources and limits

Every figure is from a government source: CBO, the Joint Committee on Taxation, the Congressional Research Service, GAO, the Treasury, the IRS, USDA, DHS and ICE. Projections are estimates. Treasury and DHS releases are written by the administration that passed the law and present its case. Government sources could not answer four things asked for: a per-household tax cut for the top 1%, the president’s personal savings, shootings by immigration agents, and the share of detainees without a criminal conviction.
