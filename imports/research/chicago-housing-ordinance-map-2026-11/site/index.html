Chicago Housing Ordinance Map

Where Chicago's housing ordinances are being broken, building by building, from the city's own open records. Each record is labeled with the section of the Municipal Code of Chicago behind it. The first three maps each show one kind of record and move together when you pan or zoom. The last map puts everything on one map.

    Method and sources

    Housing code violations are open residential (Department of Buildings, Conservation bureau) violations issued since 14 Nov 2024. Each is sorted into an ordinance area by the code section it cites and its description. A building with several shows in the most serious area, in this order: fire safety, heat and utilities, structure, nuisance and sanitation, other upkeep. Its popup lists every violation with its code section. About 7% of violations cite no section.

    "Open" is the status in the city's file. A violation stays open after repairs until a reinspection happens.

    Older vacant violations: the city mostly stopped using vacant-building codes in this dataset after 2019. About 28,000 addresses still have one marked open, mostly from 2015–2019. Many are likely stale.

    Restricted zones: 57 of the 69 precincts enacted after the 2023 ward remap are drawn on the 2023–24 precinct map. The 123 older zones used earlier precinct lines and aren't drawn.

    Datasets on data.cityofchicago.org: Building Violations (22u3-xenr), 311 Service Requests (v6vf-nfxy), Building Code Scofflaw List (crg5-4zyp), Vacant and Abandoned Buildings – Violations (kc9i-wq85), Foreclosed Rental Property (yhcw-iu53), House Share Prohibited Buildings List (7bzs-jsyj), House Share Restricted Residential Zone Precincts (8eww-pamb), and community area and ward precinct boundaries. Full records for any address are on the city's Building Records site.
